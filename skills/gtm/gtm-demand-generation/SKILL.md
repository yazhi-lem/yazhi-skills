name: gtm-demand-generation
description: Use when planning or diagnosing a demand programme — content, events, outbound, or community — and the pipeline is not growing. Helps you pick one channel to prove first, measure it honestly, and stop before adding a second one.
---

Demand generation fails in two directions: running five channels thinly so none is measurable, or running one channel for a quarter without a baseline so nobody can tell whether it worked. The discipline is to prove one channel against a baseline, then add the next. This skill owns the programme; `positioning-and-messaging` owns the message and `launch-planning` owns the launch moment.

## Channels

| Channel | Best when | Cost to prove | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content / SEO | Buyers search before they buy | 8-12 weeks | Organic sessions → qualified signups |
| Events | Buyers are concentrated in a room | 1 event | Meetings booked per attendee |
| Outbound | The buyer list is small and known | 2-4 weeks | Reply rate, meetings per 100 sends |
| Community | Buyers trust each other | 1 quarter | Active members, referrals |
| Partnerships | A partner already owns the audience | 1 deal | Co-sourced pipeline |

## Workflow

1. **Pick one channel and one metric.** "More pipeline" is not a metric; "20 qualified signups per month from organic search" is. A programme with two metrics has none.
2. **Record the baseline before starting.** Current sessions, signups, or reply rate, dated. Without a baseline, the result is an opinion.
3. **Set the minimum run time per channel** — 8-12 weeks for content, 2-4 for outbound. Killing a channel in week two proves nothing and wastes the setup.
4. **Define "qualified" once and apply it everywhere.** A signup that cannot buy is not pipeline; the definition belongs in the CRM, not in the deck.
5. **Measure cost per qualified lead, not cost per click.** A channel with cheap clicks and no qualified leads is the most expensive channel you have.
6. **Stop or double down at the review point.** If the channel misses the metric by the run time, stop it and say so; adding a second channel to compensate hides the failure.
7. **Write the result as a dated comparison**, baseline vs result, with the spend. A programme report without the spend is a story.

## Anti-patterns

- **Running five channels thinly.** None reaches the sample size to be measurable, and the budget disappears without a signal.
- **No baseline.** "Pipeline is up" compared to nothing is unfalsifiable; the dated baseline is the whole measurement.
- **Judging content in week two.** Content compounds over months; killing it early proves only that the setup was expensive.
- **Counting all signups as pipeline.** Unqualified signups inflate the number and mislead the next budget decision; use the written definition.
- **Optimising cost per click.** Cheap clicks with no qualified leads are the most expensive result in the table.
- **Adding a channel to rescue a failing one.** Hides the failure and doubles the spend; stop the first channel and say why.
