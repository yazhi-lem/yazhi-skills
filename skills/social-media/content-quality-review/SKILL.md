This skill acts as a final quality check for your social media content pipeline. It runs a pass across ALL pieces generated in a single batch to catch cross-platform repetition and generic phrasing before output is finalized.

## Step 1 — Cross-platform repetition check

Compare the opening line of every piece generated in this batch against every other piece. If two or more open with the same idea in the same sentence structure (not just similar topic — similar *construction*), rewrite all but one so each platform has a distinct entry point into the same idea.

| Check | Fail example | Pass example |
|---|---|---|
| Same opening construction | LinkedIn: "Most AI tools send your data overseas." / IG: "Most AI tools send your data overseas." | LinkedIn opens with a claim; IG opens with a question or scenario; Reel opens with a visual beat |
| Same closing CTA phrasing | Two platforms both end "Link in bio to learn more." | Each CTA matches the platform's native action (comment vs. click vs. watch) |

## Step 2 — Generic phrasing check

Flag and rewrite any line that could apply to almost any product or topic without modification. A generic line reads the same with the subject swapped out; a specific line doesn't.

1. Fails: "Get total control over your intelligence." (true of nearly any control-focused product)
2. Passes: "No foreign backdoors. No compliance gaps. No vendor lock-in." (specific, falsifiable claims)

## Step 3 — Hook strength check

Score each piece's opening line against these three questions. If it fails two or more, rewrite the hook:
1. Does it make a specific claim or ask a real question — not a rhetorical throat-clearer?
2. Would it stop a scroll on its own, without the rest of the post?
3. Is it under 12 words?

## Step 4 — Claim specificity check

Any claim without a concrete detail (a number, a named mechanism, a named risk) should either get one added from the source material, or be flagged as needing a number the requester should supply. Never invent a number to fill the gap — flag it instead.

## Step 5 — Show evidence before verdict

Before any "passed as-is" verdict can be written, produce a visible worksheet comparing the drafted pieces:
1. Quote each platform's hook side-by-side to prove they are structurally distinct.
2. Quote each major claim to prove it contains concrete, falsifiable mechanics (not generic filler).
3. If the evidence shows generic phrasing or repetition, rewrite the offending line before proceeding.

## Step 6 — Output

Return the batch with any rewritten pieces, plus a short note per platform if something was changed and why:

```text
PLATFORM: <name>
QUALITY CHECK: <"passed as-is" OR one line on what was changed and why>
```

This note is appended to the platform's block, before hashtags.

## Common failure modes

- **Reviewing pieces in isolation**: checking each platform's post individually catches weak hooks but misses cross-platform repetition, since the duplication only shows up when pieces are compared side by side.
- **Treating "on-brand" as "not generic"**: correctly applying tone rules (no emoji, direct voice) can still leave a claim generic — voice and specificity are separate checks and both need to pass.
- **Silently rewriting without a note**: fixing a weak hook or repeated opener without telling the requester what changed, making it hard to learn what "good" looks like for future manual drafts.
- **Inventing specifics to pass the check**: adding a fabricated statistic or number just to satisfy the claim-specificity check — always flag missing data instead of manufacturing it.
