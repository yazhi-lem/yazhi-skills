At 12 the body is in the middle of the fastest growth since infancy, and nearly every complaint a kid brings — can't concentrate, always tired, snapping at everyone, headaches — traces back to sleep, food, water, or movement before it traces to anything else. Check the boring four first. And change one habit at a time: five simultaneous resolutions fail together by day four.

## The numbers for this age

| Area | Target | Most common gap |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep | 9-11 hours nightly, consistent times ±30 min | Late screens; weekend shift of 3+ hours |
| Movement | 60 min moderate-to-vigorous daily | Nothing that raises the heart rate |
| Water | ~1.5-2 L/day, more in heat or sport | Sugary drinks counted as water |
| Screens (recreational) | ~2 h/day, none in the last hour before bed | Phone in the bedroom overnight |
| Fruit and veg | 5 portions/day, a portion ≈ their fist | Zero vegetables at two meals |
| Teeth | 2 min twice daily, floss once | 30-second brush, no floss at all |
| Protein at breakfast | Egg, dal, milk, curd, nuts — something | Sugar-only breakfast, crash by second period |

These are general guidance, not medical advice — anything persistent (pain, big weight change, exhaustion that sleep doesn't fix, low mood lasting weeks) goes to a doctor, not to a checklist.

## Workflow

1. **Check sleep before anything else.** Ask the actual bedtime and wake time for the last week, not the intended ones. An hour of nightly debt shows up as inattention and irritability that looks like an attitude problem.
2. **Move the phone out of the bedroom overnight.** Single highest-leverage change available to most 12-year-olds; charging it in another room removes the 1 a.m. decision entirely.
3. **Keep weekend wake time within 60-90 minutes of weekday.** Sleeping until noon on Sunday makes Monday feel like jet lag, because it is.
4. **Add, don't subtract, with food.** "Add a fruit at breakfast" gets done; "stop eating junk" gets abandoned and lands as shame. Protein at breakfast is the single best swap for morning concentration.
5. **Make movement social and fun, never a punishment for eating.** Cycling with friends, football, dance, swimming — 60 minutes doesn't need to be a workout, and framing exercise as calorie-payback is how disordered attitudes start.
6. **Handle body changes matter-of-factly.** Body odour, sweat, acne, hair, voice change, periods — daily shower, deodorant, clean clothes daily, wash face twice. Answer questions plainly, without embarrassment, and name a trusted adult or doctor for the ones beyond you.
7. **Change one habit for two weeks, tracked with a tick per day.** Two weeks of ticks beats a five-point plan. Then add the next one.

## Anti-patterns / common failure modes

- **Commenting on a 12-year-old's weight or body shape.** Reliably harmful, frequently the start of a long relationship with food shame. Talk about energy, strength, and sleep instead.
- **Debugging mood and focus without checking sleep.** Everything downstream is unmeasurable until sleep is fixed.
- **Five habits at once.** All five collapse together and the kid concludes they're the kind of person who can't stick to anything.
- **Screens allowed in the bedroom overnight.** Beats every other sleep intervention combined, in the wrong direction.
- **Framing food as good vs bad.** Creates guilt around eating without changing what gets eaten; frame it as "most days" versus "sometimes".
