Under stress a 12-year-old will execute roughly three remembered steps, so first aid for this age is a short list drilled in advance, not a manual read in the moment. The most important thing they can learn is not a technique — it's that calling for help early is always the right call, and that no one will ever be annoyed at them for calling when it turned out to be nothing.

This is basic guidance for common situations. It doesn't replace a real first-aid course or professional medical advice, and anything serious, unclear, or getting worse means calling emergency services and a trusted adult straight away.

## Know these before anything happens

| Item | What to memorise |
|---|---|
| Emergency number | Local, memorised: 112 in India and most of Europe, 911 US/Canada, 999 UK, 000 Australia |
| Own details | Full home address with landmark, and two adult phone numbers, known by heart — not just saved in a phone |
| Medical facts | Own allergies, any medicine taken, any condition an ambulance crew should know |
| Kit location | Where the first aid box, fire extinguisher, and mains switch are |

## The universal first three steps

1. **Is it safe to approach?** Fire, traffic, water, live wires — do not enter. Getting hurt while helping makes two casualties.
2. **Call for help.** Shout for the nearest adult; call the emergency number if there's no adult, or if there's blood that won't stop, breathing trouble, unconsciousness, a head or neck injury, a burn bigger than their palm, or anything they're unsure about.
3. **Then help, from the list below.** Stay on the phone; the operator will tell them what to do and is trained for exactly this.

## The short list

| Situation | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Small cut | Rinse under clean running water, press with a clean cloth until bleeding stops, cover | Don't use cotton wool directly on the wound |
| Heavy bleeding | Press hard and keep pressing, raise the limb, call for help | Don't lift the cloth to peek |
| Burn | Cool running water 20 minutes, remove rings/watch nearby, cover loosely with cling film or a clean cloth | No ice, no toothpaste, no butter, don't burst blisters |
| Choking, can cough | Encourage coughing, stay with them | Don't slap the back or grab them while they're coughing |
| Choking, silent | Shout for an adult, call emergency, 5 back blows between the shoulder blades | Don't leave them alone |
| Nosebleed | Sit, lean forward, pinch the soft part 10 minutes | Don't tilt the head back |
| Bad fall, head or neck | Don't move them, call emergency, keep them still and warm | Don't sit them up or take a helmet off |
| Sprain | Rest, ice wrapped in cloth 15 min, support, raise | Don't push through it |
| Fire | Get everyone out, close doors behind, call from outside | Never go back in for anything |
| Feeling unsafe anywhere | Leave, get to a public place or a shop, call an adult | Don't wait to be sure |

## Workflow for preparing a kid

1. **Drill the emergency number and home address until it's automatic**, including the landmark line. Phones die and screens lock.
2. **Practise the call once out loud**: where I am, what happened, how many people, my name. Practising it removes the freeze.
3. **Walk them physically to the first aid kit, extinguisher, and mains switch.** Knowing the location beats knowing the theory.
4. **Set the standing permission plainly: call anytime, for anything, no permission needed and no consequences** — the hesitation to "bother" someone is what costs minutes in real incidents.
5. **Refresh it twice a year**, in two minutes over dinner. Unrehearsed lists evaporate.

## Anti-patterns / common failure modes

- **Waiting to be sure before calling.** The dangerous minutes are the ones spent deciding; operators would far rather take a false alarm.
- **Ice or toothpaste on a burn.** Both make the injury worse; only cool running water for a full 20 minutes.
- **Moving someone after a head, neck, or back injury.** Can turn a recoverable injury into a permanent one.
- **Going back inside a fire** for a pet, a phone, or a sibling already outside — the leading cause of death in house fires.
- **Emergency numbers only in the phone.** A locked or dead phone at the moment it matters.
